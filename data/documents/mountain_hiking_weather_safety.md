## 1. High Altitude Mountain Weather Dynamics
Mountainous terrain (such as Manali, Shimla, Leh-Ladakh, Uttarakhand, and the Western Ghats) creates localized microclimates where weather changes rapidly and unpredictably.

Key principles hikers and travelers must understand:
1. **Lapse Rate:** Air temperature drops by approximately 6.5°C for every 1,000 meters of altitude gained. A pleasant 25°C in the foothills can quickly turn to near-freezing conditions at mountain passes.
2. **Rapid Convective Weather:** Clear morning skies in mountain valleys frequently transition into intense afternoon thunderstorms, hail, or dense cloud cover within 30 to 60 minutes.
3. **Wind Chill:** High wind speeds on ridges and exposed peaks strip body heat dramatically faster than still air, sharply elevating the risk of hypothermia even above 0°C.

---

## 2. When is Hiking Safe vs. Unsafe?

### Safe to Hike:
- When the forecast predicts **clear skies or light cloud cover** with low precipitation probability (<20%).
- Wind speeds on trails are below 25 km/h.
- IMD alert status for the district is **Green (No warning)**.

### Unsafe to Hike (Postpone or Cancel):
- IMD has issued a **Yellow (Watch), Orange (Alert), or Red (Warning)** for rain, thunderstorms, snow, or cloudbursts.
- Active rainfall increases the imminent risk of landslides, mudslides, falling rocks, and sudden flash floods in mountain streams (nullahs).
- Dense fog or low cloud ceiling reduces trail visibility to under 10 meters.

---

## 3. Essential Hiking Safety Checklist
Before embarking on high-altitude hikes or mountain trails:
1. **Weather Check:** Check local mountain-specific forecasts and recent precipitation history over the past 48 hours (soil saturation increases landslide risk).
2. **Clothing Layers:** Use the 3-layer rule:
   - Base layer: Moisture-wicking synthetic or merino wool (avoid cotton).
   - Insulating mid-layer: Fleece jacket or down sweater.
   - Outer shell: Fully waterproof, windproof, breathable hardshell jacket and rain pants.
3. **Gear:** Sturdy, waterproof trekking shoes with deep lug traction, hiking poles, headlamp with spare batteries, offline GPS maps, and emergency thermal bivy foil blanket.
4. **Hydration & Nutrition:** Drink 3 to 4 liters of water daily to mitigate Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS); carry high-energy snacks (nuts, dried fruit, chocolate).
5. **Stream Crossing Safety:** Never attempt to ford a swollen mountain stream during or immediately following heavy rain. Wait for water levels to recede or turn back.
