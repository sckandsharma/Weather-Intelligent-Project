# NDMA Guidelines: Management and Prevention of Heat Waves

**Source Authority:** National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), Government of India  
**Official Portal:** https://ndma.gov.in/Governance/Guidelines/Heat-Wave  
**Category:** heatwave  
**Document Version:** 1.0  

---

## 1. Definition and Criteria of Heat Wave in India
According to the India Meteorological Department (IMD) and NDMA, a heat wave is declared based on the following temperature thresholds and departures:

- **Plains:** Maximum temperature reaches at least 40°C.
- **Coastal Areas:** Maximum temperature reaches at least 37°C.
- **Hilly Regions:** Maximum temperature reaches at least 30°C.

### Criteria Based on Departure from Normal:
- **Heat Wave:** Departure from normal temperature is between +4.5°C and +6.4°C.
- **Severe Heat Wave:** Departure from normal temperature is greater than +6.4°C.

### Criteria Based on Actual Maximum Temperature (for plains):
- **Heat Wave:** Maximum temperature is 45°C or higher.
- **Severe Heat Wave:** Maximum temperature is 47°C or higher.

## 2. Health Impacts and Symptoms
Extreme heat places high stress on the human body, leading to physiological deterioration:
1. **Heat Cramps:** Painful muscle spasms usually in legs or abdomen caused by heavy sweating and loss of salts.
2. **Heat Exhaustion:** Characterized by heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, fainting, and pale, clammy skin.
3. **Heat Stroke (Medical Emergency):** Core body temperature rises above 40°C (104°F). Skin is hot, red, and dry (sweating ceases). Confusion, delirium, seizures, or loss of consciousness occur. Requires immediate hospital attention.

## 3. General Public Precautions: Dos and Don'ts

### Dos:
- Drink water frequently throughout the day, even if not thirsty.
- Consume electrolyte-rich fluids: Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS), homemade lassi, torani (rice water), lemon water, buttermilk, and coconut water.
- Wear lightweight, loose-fitting, light-colored, breathable cotton clothing.
- Use protective gear when outdoors: umbrella, wide-brimmed hat, sunglasses, or cover head with a damp cloth.
- Stay indoors during peak sunlight hours between 11:00 AM and 4:00 PM.
- Keep living spaces cool by using curtains, shades, cross-ventilation, and wet mops.

### Don'ts:
- Do not leave children, elderly persons, or pets unattended in parked vehicles.
- Avoid strenuous physical activities outdoors during peak noon hours.
- Avoid dehydrating beverages such as alcohol, excessively sugary soda, and high-caffeine drinks (coffee, tea).
- Avoid high-protein, stale, or oily heavy meals that increase metabolic body heat.

## 4. Special Precautions for Elderly and Vulnerable Populations
Elderly citizens (aged 60 and above), infants, pregnant women, and individuals with chronic cardiovascular, kidney, or pulmonary diseases are at highest risk during heatwaves:

1. **Hydration Monitoring:** Aging diminishes the sensation of thirst. Caregivers must ensure elderly persons drink water regularly on a fixed schedule.
2. **Thermal Regulation:** Older bodies have reduced sweating efficiency and slower circulation. Elderly persons should remain in the coolest, shaded room of the house, preferably with fans or evaporative cooling.
3. **Clothing:** Elderly should wear pure, airy cotton garments.
4. **Emergency First-Aid:** If an elderly individual exhibits confusion, slurred speech, lethargy, or stopped perspiration:
   - Move them immediately to a shaded or air-conditioned area.
   - Loosen tight clothing.
   - Apply cool, damp cloths or ice packs to the neck, armpits, and groin.
   - Fan vigorously while spraying cool water.
   - Contact emergency medical services immediately.
