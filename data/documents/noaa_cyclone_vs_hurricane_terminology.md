# NOAA Ocean & Atmosphere Facts: Cyclone, Hurricane, and Typhoon Terminology

**Source Authority:** National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), USA  
**Official Portal:** https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/cyclone.html  
**Category:** tropical_storms  
**Document Version:** 1.0  

---

## 1. The Core Scientific Truth
Scientifically, **a cyclone, a hurricane, and a typhoon are the exact same weather phenomenon**. 

Meteorologists classify all of them as **tropical cyclones**—rapidly rotating storm systems characterized by a low-pressure center, a closed low-level atmospheric circulation, strong spiral bands of thunderstorms, and gale-force sustained winds.

The only difference between a cyclone, a hurricane, and a typhoon is the **geographic ocean basin** where the storm is born.

---

## 2. Regional Naming by Ocean Basin

### 1. Hurricane
- **Locations:** 
  - North Atlantic Ocean (including the Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of Mexico)
  - Eastern and Central North Pacific Ocean
- **Affected Regions:** United States, Canada, Mexico, Central America, Caribbean islands, and Hawaii.

### 2. Typhoon
- **Locations:** 
  - Northwest Pacific Ocean (west of the International Date Line)
- **Affected Regions:** Japan, China, Taiwan, the Philippines, Vietnam, and Korea.

### 3. Tropical Cyclone / Cyclone
- **Locations:** 
  - Northern Indian Ocean (Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea)
  - Southwest Indian Ocean (Madagascar, Mauritius, East Africa)
  - South Pacific Ocean (Australia, Fiji, New Zealand)
- **Affected Regions:** India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Oman, Yemen, and Northern Australia.

---

## 3. Direction of Rotation
Due to the **Coriolis Effect** caused by Earth's rotation:
- In the **Northern Hemisphere** (Hurricanes, Typhoons, and North Indian Ocean Cyclones), tropical cyclones spin **counter-clockwise**.
- In the **Southern Hemisphere** (South Pacific and South Indian Ocean Cyclones), tropical cyclones spin **clockwise**.

---

## 4. Minimum Formation Requirements
Regardless of the regional name, all tropical cyclones require:
1. Warm ocean surface temperatures (at least 26.5°C or 80°F) to a depth of at least 50 meters.
2. High atmospheric moisture (humidity) in the mid-troposphere.
3. Low vertical wind shear between the surface and upper troposphere.
4. Sufficient Coriolis force (usually at least 5 degrees of latitude away from the Equator).
